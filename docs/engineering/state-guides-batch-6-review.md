## Research limits and retrieval notes

- Utah: tax research located [Bulletin 2009-4](https://insurance.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2009-4Signed.pdf)
  and the current R590-190-11 lead. The bulletin remains listed by the Department,
  but current rule text and the applicable charge categories were not fully
  verified together. No settlement-tax entitlement or absence claim is published.
  The bulletin is an image PDF and direct download returned 403. An incorrect
  rule link on the consumer page was not copied into the guide.
- Vermont: three public HTML pages returned errors through web opening, but direct
  retrieval exposed their complete substantive primary content. The inspection
  advisory contains an unfinished issuance-day label; the guide uses only its
  current consumer explanations and fixes its own actual review date. Broader
  replacement-purchase tax-credit procedures and salvage-application deadline
  exceptions are not summarized.
- Virginia: sales-tax research located vehicle transaction exemptions, which do
  not establish a general insurer settlement-tax duty. No such duty or absence is
  claimed. The specimen policy PDF was read at its actual appraisal page.
- Washington: [WSR 26-17-089](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsr/2026/17/26-17-089.htm)
  is an adopted permanent rule, effective **October 18, 2026**. Both current WAC
  pages display versions before and after that date. The guide uses the version
  in force on September 26 and should be reviewed at the effective-date change.
  The OIC complaint page's complete relevant official text was available through
  indexed web retrieval; direct page opening returned 403 and direct host access
  also failed. This retrieval limitation is not classified as a broken link.
  The complex reopening procedure is omitted rather than shortened incorrectly.
- West Virginia: the official auto filing checklist was read as published,
  including its pages on total-loss adjustments and appraisal. No fixed tax rate
  is hardcoded. Title and consumer rules are not collapsed into one threshold.
- Wisconsin: the official insurance-law PDF is the August 2026 edition even
  though a search result described the older 2025 edition. The actual PDF controls.
  The auto FAQ's broad 70% summary is not used as a universal legal threshold.
  Tax searches and the auto consumer guide did not establish a verified payment
  or replacement-purchase tax rule; no entitlement or absence claim is made.
- Wyoming: tax research found title-transfer taxation and physical-damage-rule
  leads, but did not verify a general settlement-tax duty. The guide omits that
  assertion. Coverage and rental details are identified as policy guidance.
