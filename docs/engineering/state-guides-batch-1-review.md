## Access checks and deliberate omissions

- Arizona DIFI and Colorado's live insurance/DMV pages were opened and read in a browser after automated fetch restrictions. The DIFI `#insurance` target and Colorado DMV accordion-heading target were present in the rendered document. Colorado's former `/salvage-vehicles` path redirects to `/title`; the guide links directly to the current destination.
- Colorado's current insurance-regulation listing identified the August 19, 2026 compilation, but its PDF could not be retrieved. Older archived compilations and third-party copies were not used to publish detailed methodology or timing claims. The completed guide uses independently verified official consumer guidance, DMV instructions, and the court's fee interpretation. Its practical comparable-review advice is not presented as an additional statutory requirement.
- Connecticut's legislature endpoint could not be retrieved. The department's page reproduces the relevant statutory text and was reviewed directly; the guide cites that reviewed page, not an unverified statutory anchor.
- Arkansas's separate DFA reconstruction PDF was unavailable. The guide uses the Attorney General's reviewed age-qualified description and directs readers to confirm existing out-of-state brands separately. No broad claim that an older vehicle loses an existing brand is made.
- Alaska's older claim webpage and the August 2026 policy checklist describe appraisal expenses differently. The guide does not publish the older expense allocation; it directs readers to the statute-compliant clause and a written cost explanation.
- Delaware's current arbitration page lists a $50 fee; an older rule PDF's different figure is not used. MV347's total-loss-specific provision is distinguished from ordinary sales. It does not define the one-year period's starting event; the guide explicitly directs the reader to confirm it with DMV and does not invent that event.
- No numeric Colorado salvage threshold, universal appraisal right outside the verified Alaska requirement, or unsupported replacement-purchase tax exemption is inferred from silence in the sources.
