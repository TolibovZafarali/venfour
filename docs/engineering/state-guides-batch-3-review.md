## Research limits and retrieval notes

- Louisiana: tax searches did not establish a verified motor-vehicle settlement tax
  rule. No tax entitlement or absence claim is published. The LDI newsletter and
  auto page both rendered substantive content through web retrieval; simple HTTP
  probes sometimes returned 403.
- Maine: the FAQ's approximate insurer practice was not promoted to a statutory
  percentage threshold. The newer regional statute controls the comparable-location
  discussion.
- Maryland: consumer pages differ on threshold wording, and retained-title
  treatment needs reconciliation with newer title legislation. Salvage threshold
  and retention assertions are omitted. The unavailable `publicnew` PDF is not
  linked; the verified lowercase `consumer/documents/publications` destination is.
- Massachusetts: web retrieval returned 403, but direct retrieval provided full
  primary content for the three published sources. A proposed appraisal regulation
  was excluded. Revenue's vehicle-tax guide was inspected, but a general
  replacement allowance or insurer tax obligation was not established and is not
  asserted.
- Michigan: published older guides remain linked from DIFS, but this module uses
  only their policy-document and claims-practice explanations. Searches did not
  establish a current total-loss tax entitlement. The title classification formula
  and auto appraisal mandates are not asserted.
- Minnesota: the `www.commerce.mn.gov` host had retrieval/certificate problems;
  the published `mn.gov` destination works. The settlement statute was also read
  directly after web retrieval failed. The self-insured damage threshold was not
  generalized to all insured vehicles.
- Mississippi: tax research did not establish a verified total-loss payment or
  replacement-purchase rule. The guide makes neither claim. A proposed 2010 bill
  found in search was excluded.
- Montana: salvage road-use and retitling provisions were verified directly.
  Model-year eligibility and settlement-fee obligations are not generalized from
  the title rule or the absence of a general sales tax.
