# State guide pilot source review

Sources checked: **September 26, 2026** (America/Chicago).

This review covers the California, Texas, and District of Columbia educational
content modules. Sources were opened and their relevant sections read. Dates in
the modules record this review; they are not generated at page load. Public copy
uses concise paraphrases and links beside the supported paragraph. Shared intake,
pricing, refund, and service limitations remain in the common guide renderer.

## California

| Source | Reviewed location |

| [Claims guidance](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/hadaccident.cfm) | Actual cash value and appraisal FAQs; Important Tips; fair-claims overview |

Qualifications preserved: policy-dependent value definition and appraisal;
settlement components kept separate from vehicle value; title classification kept
separate from settlement calculation; mediation restricted to eligible personal
physical-damage claims, with both amount thresholds and excluded claim types.

The [regulations index](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/130-laws-regs-hearings/05-CCR/fair-claims-regs.cfm)
now links to an external official publisher. The section 2695.8 destination
returned an access error in both browser retrieval and a direct request. The
guide therefore cites the department's readable consumer explanation and does
not reproduce detailed regulatory valuation methods or deadlines from an
unverified copy. Unrelated accident-reporting thresholds and liability limits in
the consumer guide were not used.

| [Total-loss consumer guidance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/car-totaled.html) | Total-loss decision; local evidence; retained salvage |

| [DMV classification guidance](https://www.txdmv.gov/dealers/licensing/salvage-dealer) | Opening salvage and nonrepairable paragraphs |

Qualifications preserved: specified repair costs and exclusions; title definition
distinguished from the insurer's total-loss decision; replacement-purchase tax
distinguished from amounts owed in an insurance settlement; appraisal limited to
the policyholder's insurer. The public content includes the January 1, 2026
policy-date applicability of the new appraisal provision requirement.

The requirement was cross-checked against [TDI's July 2026 bulletin](https://agate.tdi.texas.gov/bulletins/2026/commissioners-bulletin-b-0006-26.html).
The enrolled act supplies the exact **on or after** date language. The statute's
September 1, 2025 effective date is not confused with its policy-date applicability.
Proposed appraisal process rules were not treated as adopted requirements, and no
new procedural deadlines were inferred. The older statutes HTML address rendered
a navigation page, so the published citation uses the verified enrolled act.

## District of Columbia

| Source | Reviewed location |
| --- | --- |
| [DISB auto claims guidance](https://disb.dc.gov/page/things-know-about-car-insurance-and-rental-cars-starting-your-road-trip) | Total loss; rental reimbursement |
| [Salvage definition](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/50-1331.01) | Paragraph (12)(A)–(B) |
| [Salvage title duties](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/50-1331.02) | Subsections (a), (c), and (d) |
| [DISB-published consumer guide](https://disb.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/disb/publication/attachments/publication-aut-pp-consumer-auto.pdf#page=15) | PDF page 15, printed page 9, Filing a Claim |
| [Complaint process](https://disb.dc.gov/service/file-complaint-or-report-fraud) | Complaint scope; written dispute; Consumer Services |

Qualifications preserved: **exceeds 75%**, historic-vehicle exception, prescribed
retail-value reference, and voluntary salvage designation; distinct owner,
lessor, and insurer triggering events; rental discussion confined to the
customer's own coverage. The national consumer guide is identified as policy
guidance, not a District-wide appraisal entitlement.

The DMV summary and current Code use different triggering language for an
insurer's title application. The guide follows the current Code. It does not
repeat the consumer page's suggestion that every insurer uses a particular
valuation vendor.

Replacement-tax research reached the [DMV exemption chart](https://dmv.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/dmv/publication/attachments/Amended_Excise_Tax_Exemption_Categories_2.5.25.pdf).
It distinguishes defective-vehicle replacements and insurance-company title
transfers. That source did not establish a general customer total-loss replacement
allowance or settle insurer tax reimbursement. Neither proposition is asserted in
the guide; absence of a located rule is not represented as absence of a right.

## Integration review

Each module uses the shared content interface, contains jurisdiction-specific
valuation, three rule topics, reconsideration guidance, and two localized FAQs.
Every public legal proposition has a source reference. The modules contain no
valuation calculations, operating approvals, payment decisions, or backend
research imports. This document records editorial review only; rendering and
route verification are completed in the shared state-guide test suite.
